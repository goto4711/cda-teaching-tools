1. Data critique: Global Aid Alliance

Machine learning learns from data, so its results can only be as good as the data and the choices we make about it. Before we cluster and classify, we practise data critique: asking what a dataset is, who made it, what it leaves out, and what happens when we turn it into a label (what a model predicts) and features (what it predicts from).

The case. A disaster relief NGO, Global Aid Alliance, has exported five years of donation records. It wants to understand donation patterns, improve fundraising efficiency and increase donor retention. Can the data do this?

2. Handout 1: Data sheet for donor_records_export.csv

Global Aid Alliance is a fictional organisation; the data are made up for teaching. Read the sheet as if it were real: it is the organisation's own account of its data, and like any such account it may be incomplete or wrong.

From: Fundraising & Development, Global Aid Alliance
Purpose of the export: to understand donation patterns, improve fundraising efficiency and increase donor retention.

What the file contains

  • All individual donations from 1 January 2020 to 31 December 2024, exported from our Donor Management System (DonorBase CRM) on 15 January 2025.
  • Export filter: individual donors only, completed gifts of $10 or more. Refunds and failed payments are excluded. Corporate partners are kept in a separate system and are not included.
  • One row per donation. Every donor has a unique donor ID, so repeat gifts can be linked to the same person.

How the data are collected

  • Online: donors fill in the form on our website. Age range and gender are optional questions on the form.
  • Mail: cheques are opened and typed into the CRM by volunteers. Where the donor is already known, volunteers copy age range, gender and region from the donor's earlier record.
  • Event: donations at fundraising events are written on paper forms and entered later by staff.

5. The full checklist for your group project

Use this for the data your group collects. The ten questions above are the core; these are the questions behind them. Answer, evidence and confidence apply to every question.

Context and provenance

  • What is the data source (database, API, manual entry, sensors, survey, scraping)?
  • Who owns or maintains the dataset, and who made it for what purpose?
  • What process or system generates the data?
  • What does each row represent (granularity)?
  • What period does the data cover? For internet data: when exactly was it collected?
  • How was it collected? Was software used, and with which settings?
  • Who or what is not in the data, and why?
  • Whose question does the data answer, and whose can it not answer?

Structure and schema

  • What are the data types, and are they correctly assigned?
  • Which columns are raw and which derived or computed, and by whom?
  • Are the column names clear, and is there a data sheet or dictionary? Does the data match it?
  • Which categories were designed by someone (gender, region, age bands), and what can't be said in them?

Quality and integrity

  • How much is missing per column, and is missingness patterned (by time, group, channel)?
  • Are there disguised missing values ("unknown", 0, 1900-01-01)?
  • Are there duplicates or inconsistent records? Do IDs identify what they claim to?
  • Are values within expected ranges and units, and are categories valid?
  • Are timestamps valid, and do events fit what you know about the world?

Statistics and distributions

  • What are the summary statistics and distribution shapes?
  • Are classes or groups imbalanced?
  • Are there correlations between features?
  • Are there time trends or seasonality?
  • Are there outliers, and are they errors or interesting?

The label

  • What is the label (target), who defined it, and how was it measured?
  • Is it a proxy? What does it record, and what does it miss?
  • Was it set by a threshold or a human judgement? Who chose it, and what changes if it moves?
  • Are there enough examples of each class?

The features

  • Which columns become features, and which encoding choices does that require?
  • Is a feature itself the output of another model or score?
  • Is a feature sensitive, or a stand-in for something sensitive?
  • Leakage: is any feature known only after, or because of, the label?
  • Which important features are missing?

Ethics and interpretation

  • Could the data encode bias (sampling, measurement, social)?
  • Which privacy and legal standards apply (GDPR)? Does the stated legal basis cover your use?
  • Who could be affected by the analysis or predictions, and could it cause harm or discrimination?

Readiness

  • Which cleaning and transformation steps are needed, and which of them are interpretations?
  • Is the data fresh enough, and updated often enough?
  • Which assumptions must hold for the analysis to be valid?
  • Can this data answer the question at all?
