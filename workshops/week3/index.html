1. Data critique: ten questions for structured data

Every dataset was made by someone, for some purpose, about some people. This week is about structured data: tables of rows and columns, as in a spreadsheet or a database. Before we analyse such a table, and before a machine learns from it, we ask what it is, who made it, who is in it and who is left out, and what happens when its columns become a label (what a model predicts) and features (what it predicts from).

Use the ten questions below on every table of data you work with: the Senate votes and Spotify songs in the next hour, and the tables in your group project. Today we practise them on one case, the donation records of a (fictional) NGO. Next week is about text, where some of the questions change.

Mostly thinking, not coding. Most questions are answered by reading the data sheet, looking at the rows and discussing. For each answer, say what it rests on: the data sheet says …, we saw in the data …, or we assume …. Code is there if you want to check a suspicion.

2. The practice case: Global Aid Alliance

A disaster relief NGO, Global Aid Alliance, has exported five years of donation records. It wants to understand donation patterns, improve fundraising efficiency and increase donor retention: keeping donors giving. Can the data do this, and should it be used this way?

Global Aid Alliance is a fictional organisation; the data are made up for teaching. Read the data sheet as if it were real: it is the organisation's own account of its data, and like any such account it may be incomplete or wrong.

Data sheet for donor_records_export.csv

From: Fundraising & Development, Global Aid Alliance
Purpose of the export: to understand donation patterns, improve fundraising efficiency and increase donor retention.

What the file contains

  • All individual donations from 1 January 2020 to 31 December 2024, exported from our Donor Management System (DonorBase CRM) on 15 January 2025.
  • Export filter: individual donors only, completed gifts of $10 or more. Refunds and failed payments are excluded. Corporate partners are kept in a separate system and are not included.
  • One row per donation. Every donor has a unique donor ID, so repeat gifts can be linked to the same person.

How the data are collected

  • Online: donors fill in the form on our website. Age range and gender are optional questions on the form.
  • Mail: cheques are opened and typed into the CRM by volunteers. Where the donor is already known, volunteers copy age range, gender and region from the donor's earlier record.
  • Event: donations at fundraising events are written on paper forms and entered later by staff.

4. The full audit checklist for your group project

The ten questions are the core. This is the full Structured Data Audit Checklist from the lecture: use it for the data your group collects, before any analysis or modelling.

Context & provenance

  • What is the data source (database, API, manual entry, sensors, survey, scraping …)?
  • Who owns or maintains the dataset?
  • What process or system generates the data?
  • What does each row represent (granularity)?
  • What period does the data cover? For internet data: when exactly was it collected?
  • How was the data collected? Are there known biases or gaps?
  • Was software used to collect the data? If so, with which settings?
  • What is the goal or question behind the dataset, and whose question is it?
  • Who or what is not in the data, and why?

Structure & schema

  • What are the data types (numeric, categorical, datetime …), and are they correctly assigned?
  • Which columns are raw, and which are derived or computed, and by whom?
  • Are the column names consistent and clear? Is there a data sheet or dictionary, and does the data match it?
  • Which categories did someone design (gender, region, age bands, genres), and what can't be said in them?

Data quality & integrity

  • Are there missing values? In what proportion per column?
  • Are there patterns in missingness (by time, category, group)? Are there disguised missing values ("unknown", 0, 1900-01-01)?
  • Are there duplicates or inconsistent records? Do IDs identify what they claim to?
  • Are numeric values within expected ranges and units? Are categorical values valid?
  • Are there impossible or implausible entries? Are timestamps valid, and do events fit what you know about the world?
  • Is there evidence of data leakage (the target known in the features)?

Statistical & distributional checks

  • What are the summary statistics (mean, median, min, max, standard deviation)?
  • What is the shape of the distributions?
  • Are classes or groups imbalanced?
  • Are there correlations among features?
  • Are there time trends or seasonality?
  • Are there anomalies or outliers? How many, and are they errors or interesting?

Ethical & interpretive considerations

  • Could the dataset encode bias (sampling, measurement, social)?
  • Does it include sensitive attributes (gender, ethnicity, religion, age, location …), or things that stand in for them?
  • Who are the people in the data? Did they know about it, and did they agree to this use?
  • What privacy, legal or compliance standards apply (GDPR)? Could someone be identified?
  • Could analysis or models based on this data cause harm or discrimination?
  • Who might be affected by insights or predictions, and who benefits?

Readiness for analysis / modelling

  • What cleaning or transformation steps are needed, and which of them are interpretations?
  • What is the candidate target variable (label), who defined it, and is it a proxy?
  • Are there enough examples per class or group?
  • Are key features missing? Is any feature itself the output of another model?
  • Is the data fresh enough, and updated often enough?
  • What assumptions must hold for the analysis to be valid?
